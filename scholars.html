Why it matters

Most patients navigate care without a guide.

About 85% of U.S. cancer patients are treated in the community or at home, far from the support of a comprehensive cancer center. Arul pairs AI agents that take action with trained human navigators — so patients aren’t left to figure it out alone.

What’s the difference between a co-op and an internship, anyway?

Both are professional work experiences that set you up for what’s next. A co-op goes further: more time, more ownership and a real seat on the team.

Arul co-op
Length

A full semester, full-time — long enough to dig into the work.

Ownership

You start projects and see them through to launch.

Contribution

You’re a true team member, solving real problems for patients and partners.

Mentorship

An on-site supervisor sets goals with you and reviews your progress.

Pay

Paid — your work is valued like the rest of the team’s.

Meet our founder

Eshan Vishwakarma

Founder & CEO, Arul Health

“At a young age I was diagnosed with a rare form of cancer that I was never expected to survive — stage 3 neuroblastoma at the age of four. The reason I survived is because my parents became my full-time care navigators, advocating for everything. We shifted our entire lives for my chemotherapy, radiation and immunotherapy. It was only by chance that my family found Memorial Sloan Kettering, where I joined an immunotherapy trial that saved my life. The trial worked, but reaching it took a level of persistence, resourcefulness, and second-opinion-seeking that not every family is positioned to manage. And as a survivor, I’m committing my life, single-mindedly, to reversing the structural asymmetry that defines the U.S. healthcare system that folks from socially and economically disadvantaged backgrounds know all too well, using tech to close the care gaps and uplift others on their journeys.”

— Eshan Vishwakarma

A Harvard economics graduate, Eshan is a Social Innovation + Change Initiative Fellow at Harvard Kennedy School, a Roberts Family Technology Innovation Fellow and a Cancer Nation Advocate and Ambassador. Before Arul, he founded Outspoken Narrative, a nonprofit film company that produced 12 documentaries with student filmmakers worldwide, and tutored kids in treatment through ASK Childhood Cancer Foundation, which named him its 2022 Volunteer of the Year.
